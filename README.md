# Gap Analysis for the STEAM Ahead Project, Dolpa, Nepal

## What this report is
A gap analysis (needs assessment) prepared before implementation of STEAM Ahead, a project led by Plan International Nepal with partners Women in STEAM, Open Learning Exchange Nepal, and Social Awareness Center Nepal, funded by the European Union. It covers 50 schools across six rural municipalities in Dolpa district, Karnali Province. The study looked at the state of education in Dolpa to plan Women in STEAM's activities: capacity training for local officials and civil society groups, makerspaces in six schools, teacher training, a STEAM training manual, and orientations for school management and parent-teacher committees.

## Method
Field visit observations, literature review, secondary data analysis, and interviews with students, teachers, parents and locals, education officers, local government officials, and I/NGO and civil society staff. It also used Plan International's baseline data. Inputs came from a project start-up meeting in Dunai and a team reflection meeting after the visit.

## Key findings
- **Remote and hard to reach:** Dolpa is Nepal's largest district by area, with very low population density, limited roads, and unreliable electricity and internet.
- **Seasonal school closures:** Schools in the northern highlands close for about six months each year. A cited 2010 study found about 83 school days lost per year to seasonal absenteeism in Karnali.
- **Low education levels:** About 43% of the population is illiterate and about 8% has passed secondary education. Enrollment falls at higher grade levels.
- **Teaching:** Teachers are in short supply, often teach several subjects, and rely on rote and chalkboard methods. They report too few materials and too little training for practical science and math.
- **Digital learning:** Computer practical sessions are limited. Donor-provided resources have often not lasted because of supply and logistics.
- **Gender and social barriers:** Plan International's 2019 baseline, cited in the report, found that 42% of girls do not feel encouraged to take leadership roles in school and 44% of girls and 59% of boys do not feel teachers support them. The report also notes caste discrimination in schools, early marriage, and the view that girls' education matters less.
- **Opportunity:** Locally available materials such as wood, stones, and plants can support STEAM teaching, and communities welcome efforts to improve education.

## Recommendations
- Coordinate closely with consortium partners and local authorities.
- Plan around winter access, seasonal school closures, and local elections.
- Choose makerspace sites that have space, electricity, and internet, and plan transport early.
- Build STEAM materials and teacher training around local resources, culture, and current teacher and student competency.
- Budget carefully. The report includes a SWOT analysis of Women in STEAM's readiness.
